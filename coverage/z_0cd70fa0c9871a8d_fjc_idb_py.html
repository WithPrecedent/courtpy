1"""Adds the Federal Judicial Center's Integrated Database to cases. 

2 

3The Federal Judicial Center's Integrated Database (IDB) has a record of each 

4appeal that the federal courts of appeals have docketed, terminated, or had 

5pending since the 2008 fiscal year, taken from the courts' own dockets: the 

6type of appeal, the parties, the court and judge appealed from, the dates of 

7each step, the judges of the panel and the opinions each wrote, how the 

8appeal ended, and whether the decision was published, among much else. It 

9covers the twelve regional circuits (not the Federal Circuit). Its codebooks 

10(https://www.fjc.gov/research/idb) explain each variable. 

11 

12courtpy uses the fjc-idb package (https://github.com/WithPrecedent/fjc_idb) 

13to download, read, and decode the database's files. `download` gets the 

14yearly files that are missing and keeps them outside of projects (in 

15"fjc_idb" in `courtpy.utilities.data_folder()`, in the layout that fjc-idb 

16uses). `appeals` returns the database's table of appeals for a range of 

17years, with fjc-idb's descriptive names for its columns (such as 

18"appeal_type", "docket_date", and "judgment_date") and labels in place of 

19many of its codes. With `district = True`, each appeal from a district court 

20also has the district court's record of the case that was appealed (in 

21columns that begin with "district_"), which fjc-idb's `merge_district` 

22finds. 

23 

24`add_idb` (an `amos.Merger`, which is added to the `amos` library as soon 

25as courtpy is imported) adds the database's columns, with "idb_" before 

26their names, by circuit and docket number. The database has a record for 

27each docket, so it is best added to `Dockets` (which `cases_to_dockets` 

28makes), each of whose rows has one docket number ("docket"). A case that 

29consolidated several appeals then has each appeal's record on its own row: 

30 

31```python 

32import courtpy 

33 

34cases = courtpy.parse("data/courtlistener") 

35dockets = courtpy.code(cases, ["cases_to_dockets", "add_idb"], parameters = { 

36 "add_idb": {"columns": "appeal_type, judgment_date, published"}}) 

37``` 

38 

39Added to `Cases`, a case with several docket numbers gets the record of the 

40first of them that the database has. 

41 

42`load_idb` and `download_idb` (reference loaders, see 

43`courtpy.loaders.ReferenceLoader`) start a study of the appeals themselves, 

44whether or not they have opinions. 

45 

46Contents: 

47 AddIdb: adds the Integrated Database's record of each appeal to its 

48 case. 

49 DownloadIdb: downloads the Integrated Database's files and loads its 

50 appeals. 

51 CompareWithIdb: compares a dataset with the Integrated Database's 

52 records of appeals, and its appeals that the dataset lacks. 

53 LoadIdb: loads the Integrated Database's table of appeals for a range of 

54 years. 

55 appeals: returns the database's table of appeals for a range of years. 

56 download: downloads the database's yearly files that are missing. 

57 logger: the module's logger, which reports the files that are 

58 downloaded. 

59 _BY: the columns of the database that the groups of `compare_with_idb` 

60 name. 

61 _DOCKET_COLUMN: the column of `Dockets` with each row's one docket 

62 number. 

63 _FALSE_TEXT: text that is false in a column of flags. 

64 _TRUE_TEXT: text that is true in a column of flags. 

65 _VARIABLES: the variables that `compare_with_idb` compares, by name.

447class CompareWithIdb(amos.describers.Describer): 

448 """Compares a dataset with the Integrated Database's records of appeals. 

449 

450 The cases are made `Dockets` first (see `cases_to_dockets`), unless they 

451 are already, since the database has a record for each docket rather than 

452 for each decision. Each docket is matched to its record as `add_idb` 

453 matches it, and two tables are made. 

454 

455 The first, stored under the technique's name, says how well the 

456 variables that both have agree, one row for each variable, for the 

457 dockets that were matched: how many dockets have both values ("compared"), 

458 how many of those agree ("matches", "match_share", and Cohen's "kappa"), 

459 and the disagreements in each direction ("data_only" is true in the data 

460 and false in the database, and "idb_only" the reverse). The variables 

461 are true-or-false. By default, they are each of these whose column the 

462 data has: 

463 

464 | Variable | The data's column | The database's column | 

465 | --- | --- | --- | 

466 | criminal | "type_criminal" | "appeal_type" is a criminal appeal | 

467 | civil | "type_civil" | "appeal_type" is not a criminal appeal | 

468 | reversal | "outcome_reversal" | "disposition" is reversed (in whole or part) or remanded | 

469 | published | "published" | "published" | 

470 | dissent | "dissents" (more than 0) | "dissents" (more than 0) | 

471 | us_appellant | the United States is a party ("party1_united_states") and the appellant ("party1_appellant"), for either party | "us_appellant" | 

472 | pro_se_appellant | "pro_se_appellant" | "pro_se_appellant" | 

473 | prisoner_appeal | "prisoner_appeal" | "prisoner_appeal" | 

474 

475 The second, stored under the technique's name and "_missing", compares 

476 the appeals in the database that the data has with those that it lacks, 

477 in each circuit (or district, or any column of the database) and in all 

478 of them ("All"). The database has every appeal, so the data always lacks 

479 some. What matters is whether the appeals it lacks differ from those it 

480 has, such as whether reversals are more common among them. The appeals 

481 are those of the data's circuits whose judgments are between the data's 

482 first and last decisions and, unless "merits_only" is false, that were 

483 decided on the merits (those with a "disposition"), since the others 

484 rarely have opinions. Each row is one variable in one group: how many 

485 appeals there are ("appeals"), how many the data has ("in_data") and 

486 their share ("coverage"), the share of the appeals with a known value 

487 that are true among those the data has ("share_in_data") and those it 

488 lacks ("share_missing"), their "difference", and the p value of a 

489 two-proportion z test of it ("p_value"). Its variables are the 

490 database's side of the variables above, by default. 

491

493 `idb_missing_differences` the second (stored in `figures` under the 

494 tables' names).
