    Changelog¶

    All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

    0.6.1¶

    Changed¶

    • code_parties finds who appealed in cases whose caption gives the parties no roles ("United States v. Bullock"), when one party is the United States. The opening of the opinion decides: the role that it gives the United States or the defendant, or else a sentence that says who appeals. Before, such appeals had no "appeal_by_defendant" and no winner by side, and a study that filtered on them left them out. In the CourtListener criminal appeals of the twelve regional circuits since 2010, this finds who appealed in 9,707 of the 11,509 with no roles (9,606 by the defendant), most of them affirmances, Anders cases, and the Seventh Circuit's orders.
    • New general header rules make the columns that it uses: "opening_united_states_role", "opening_defendant_appellant", "opening_defendant_appellee", "opening_government_appeals", and "opening_party_appeals". The cases have to be parsed again to have them.
    • More panels are found whole. In the CourtListener criminal appeals of the twelve regional circuits since 2010, 2,912 more have three judges who are all found (110,941 of 119,330, up from 108,029), and none has fewer:
    • The panel rule reads the Seventh Circuit's lists of judges, which have only spaces between them ("Before JOHN DOE, Circuit Judge JANE ROE, Circuit Judge ..."), and so found one judge where there were three.
    • The names rule leaves out words that sources put among the judges' names but are not names: dispositions ("Dismissed"), "Curium", and the words of courts' names ("Eastern", "Ninth").
    • Roster.match repairs a name that fits no judge, if it then fits exactly one: small capitals ("P OSNER"), broken hyphens, a word of the panel run into a name, the last word of a last name of several words ("EVE" for St. Eve), a first and last name run together, or a scanning error of one letter in a name of five or more ("KRAVTTCH"), among the judges of the case's court. Roster.candidates returns every judge that a name fits, and Roster.full_name and Roster.name_words the ways of writing a judge's name. edit_distance and repaired_names are new functions of courtpy.judges.
    • code_panels reads a panel's names together: a last name that two judges of the court share (such as "PRYOR" in the Eleventh Circuit) is the judge whose first name is another word of the panel (as CourtListener's list of judges gives them) or whose full name is the author's, and words that are only a judge's first name are not judges of their own. It rewrites "panel_judges" and counts "panel_size" again, each judge once.
    • Judge Forrest of the Ninth Circuit is found by her earlier name, Danielle J. Hunsaker. The roster has to be made again to have it.
    • CourtListener.fetch_pdf_headers downloads the courts' own PDFs of saved cases and saves the text of each first page in the case's file as "pdf_header", which read_case makes a section and the rule that finds the panel reads after the opinion's opening. CourtListener's text of many opinions lacks the header with the panel, and its list of judges may give only last names that two judges share (such as the Ninth Circuit's two Judges Smith), so the PDF tells them apart. The Ninth and Eleventh Circuits' moved PDFs are found at their new addresses. courtpy needs pypdf. Of the 6,538 criminal appeals above whose panel was still not found whole and whose case has a PDF's address, 4,866 PDFs were read (1,111 were not found), and with them 114,129 of the 119,330 have three judges who are all found.

    0.6.0¶

    Added¶

    • download_courtlistener is a new loader, like download_idb and the other "download_" techniques: it downloads CourtListener cases into its source folder and then parses and codes them. Its "method" is "bulk" (the default), which extracts the cases from the bulk data and needs no key, or "api", which downloads them with the API. Its other parameters are courts, start_date, end_date, max_cases, refresh, dockets (for "api"), and bulk_folder, bulk_date, and stream (for "bulk").

    Changed¶

    • courtpy needs amos 0.10.3 or later. That version compares classifiers by the Matthews correlation coefficient wherever amos chooses the metric, and adds the gpboost, bambi, and pymc models.
    • CourtListener downloads from the bulk data as well as the API, and BulkData is gone. CourtListener.download takes method = "bulk" (the default) or "api". BulkData's fields are now bulk_folder, bulk_date, stream, and bulk_url, and the API's address is api_url. Its methods are bulk_dates (was available), fetch_bulk_file (fetch), read_bulk_chunks (chunks), read_bulk_rows (rows), and resolve_bulk_date (resolve_date).
    • CourtListener looks for the API key only when it first makes a request to the API, so the bulk data needs no key, and it sends the key with requests to the API alone, not in the session's headers.
    • load_court_listener is now load_courtlistener, and it only parses and codes the cases already in its folder. Use download_courtlistener to download them first.
    • overwrite is now refresh in CourtListener.download, in download_courtlistener, and in courtpy download ("--refresh"), as in the other "download_" techniques.
    • courtpy download takes "--method bulk" (the default) or "--method api" in place of the "bulk" and "api" subcommands, and its folder defaults to "courtlistener".
    • The group of courts "federal_circuits" is now "federal_geographic_circuits" (the twelve regional circuits, without the Federal Circuit).
    • CITATION.cff and the README's citation give version 0.6.0.

    Removed¶

    • courtpy.Download, courtpy.sources.DOWNLOADS, and courtpy.sources.find_download, with BulkDownload and ApiDownload. To download in a way of its own, a study subclasses CourtListener or download_courtlistener's class, DownloadCourtlistener.
    • The courtpy.bulk module, whose code is now part of courtpy.courtlistener.

    0.5.2¶

    Changed¶

    • The README's table of techniques lists the artist's plots after the critic's checks and inferer.
    • CITATION.cff and the README's citation give version 0.5.2.

    Fixed¶

    • code_parties now decides who brought an appeal from each party's own caption first. A party whose caption names it both appellant and appellee (as a caption that joins several appeals does: "Joseph Prochilo, Defendant, Appellee; United States of America, Appellant, v. Elvis Guerrero, Defendant, Appellee") takes the opposite of the other party's role. Before, both parties were left unknown. A party named appellant or appellee is no longer judged by "Petitioner" or "Respondent", so "George H. Bennett, Petitioner, Appellee" is the appellee. Two appellants (a cross-appeal) are still unknown.
    • code_case_type finds the prosecution when both parties' text names the United States, as a caption that joins several appeals can. The party whose text begins with the United States is the prosecution, and otherwise the party that is a defendant and not a plaintiff is the criminal defendant. Before, neither was the criminal defendant, so appeal_by_defendant was missing.
    • "Cross Appellee" and "Cross Appellant" written with a space, an en dash, or an em dash are cross roles. They are no longer read as plain appellees and appellants as well.
    • Recoding the 352,343 federal appellate decisions of 2010–2026 in CourtListener this way found the side of 1,979 criminal appeals that had none: 135 brought by the government and 1,844 by the defendant. It changed no side that had been found. Compared with the Integrated Database (compare_with_idb), the share of government appeals found rose from 78% to 85%.

    0.5.1¶

    Added¶

    • compare_with_idb (an explorer, in courtpy.fjc_idb) compares a study with the Federal Judicial Center's Integrated Database. Cases are compared as Dockets and matched as add_idb matches them. The table compare_with_idb shows how the variables that both have agree (whether an appeal is criminal or civil, was reversed, was published, had a dissent, was brought by the United States, had a pro se appellant, or was a prisoner's appeal, or any pair of columns): the dockets compared, the matches and Cohen's kappa, and the disagreements in each direction. The table compare_with_idb_missing compares the database's appeals that the study lacks with those it has, in each circuit (or district, with by). It reports the coverage, the shares of each variable, their difference, and a two-proportion z test. By default, it counts only appeals decided on the merits in the study's circuits and dates (merits_only).
    • The plots idb_confusion_matrices (a confusion matrix for each variable) and idb_missing_differences (the differences by group, starred below alpha), which compare_with_idb draws with visualize.

    Changed¶

    • Requires fjc-idb 0.1.0 (it was 0.0.4), whose appellate table has pro_se_appellant, prisoner_appeal, reversed_in_whole_or_part, and the other outcome columns.

    Fixed¶

    • "OWEN" in a Fifth Circuit opinion is Judge Priscilla Richman, who sat as Priscilla Owen until 2019 (the Federal Judicial Center's files give only her name now). It had been matched to Judge Richard Owen of the Southern District of New York through 2015, and to no judge after.
    • A name that fits no judge is tried in shorter forms (shorter_names): the first and last names, and then the first initial and the last name, but never the last name alone. So "Susan P. Graber" (whom the files name Susan Graber), "Sarah A. L. Merriam" (Sarah Ann Leilani Merriam), and "Ed Carnes" (Edward Carnes) are matched.

    0.5.0¶

    courtpy follows the amos way of working more closely: its data are amos datasets of one kind of row each, and everything it does to them is an amos technique that a settings file can name. Each source of cases has header rules of its own, which find courts and agencies with the tables in "courts.csv" and "agencies.csv", and courtpy has plots, checks, and an inferer for amos's "artist" and "critic" stages. Breaking throughout; no old names are kept.

    • Upgraded locked dependencies with security fixes to clear Dependabot alerts: pillow 13.0.0, filelock 4.2.12, certifi 2026.10.23, virtualenv 21.15.9, setuptools 84.2.1, gitpython 3.2.1, black 26.10.1, and uv 0.13.9.
    • "instructions/federal/courts.csv" uses levels that state courts can share. Breaking: the level "district" is now "trial" and "supreme" is now "high".
    • Breaking: the Supreme Court's court is "scotus" (it was "supreme_court"), and the column of CourtListener's ids is courtlistener_name (it was courtlistener).
    • "courts.csv" has a new feature_name column, the court's name as a label for tables and plots (such as "District of Alabama" or "SCOTUS").
    • Breaking: the group of CourtListener courts for the Supreme Court is scotus (it was supreme_court).
    • Each source has its own header rules, in "instructions/sources/{source}/header.csv" (the Lexis-Nexis rules that divide a case are in "sources/lexis_nexis" too). "general/header.csv" keeps only the rules that fit every source. A Source names its header rulebook in header ("courtlistener_header" or "lexis_nexis_header"), which the parser applies before the others. Breaking: the "header" rulebook is no longer part of "federal", options._REQUIRED_RULEBOOKS is only "general", and Parser.structures is now Parser.source_rules.
    • The header rules name no courts or agencies of their own.
    • A Lexis-Nexis case's court_num comes from the names of the courts in the jurisdiction's "courts.csv" (opinion_name, then fjc_name; see rules.make_court_rules), and a CourtListener case's from CourtListener's id for its court (courts.court_numbers_by_courtlistener_name). The judges' roster numbers its courts with the same rules.
    • "courts.csv" has opinion_name patterns for the courts of appeals and the Supreme Court, and rows for the Temporary Emergency Court of Appeals (105) and the bankruptcy appellate panels (106 to 111). Breaking: they were 998 and 999.
    • Breaking: the agency column is gone. The agencies of the jurisdiction's "agencies.csv" that each party and the history name are party1_agencies, party2_agencies, and history_agencies (see rules.make_agency_rules). In a party, an agency's name must end the party or come before a comma, semicolon, closing parenthesis, or "et al.", so a company named for an agency ("FMC WYOMING CORPORATION") is not taken for it.
    • An abbreviation in "agencies.csv" (such as "CAB" or "FMC") no longer matches right after a hyphen, where it is usually a judge's initials at the end of a docket number ("06-CR-126-CAB"), not an agency.
    • Cases have court_name (courtpy's name for the court in "courts.csv", such as "tenth_circuit") beside court_num, and lower_court_num and lower_court_name for the trial or specialized court whose decision is reviewed. The header rules find the court below in the history, by the names in "courts.csv"; a CourtListener case has it from its docket's "appeal_from" when the docket names it. The opinion_name of each district court also matches the forms of Lexis-Nexis histories ("D. N.M.", "D. of Kan.", and "W.D. Oklahoma").
    • Tighter header rules:
    • party1_united_states and party2_united_states are the United States itself ("UNITED STATES OF AMERICA", "U.S.", or "USA", also as a relator's plaintiff), not a company or official named for it (such as "U.S. Bank" or "United States Marshal").
    • docket_criminal and docket_civil need a criminal or civil docket number ("06-CR-126", "4:07CV00123", or "Criminal Action No. 07-123"), not any word that starts with "cr" or "civ".
    • future_grant needs a form of "grant" (not "approve" or "issued").
    • The Lexis-Nexis concurring_lines and dissenting_lines are only lines that start with "CONCUR" or "DISSENT" in capitals (such as "CONCUR BY:").
    • courtpy has techniques for amos's "artist" and "critic" stages.
    • Plots (courtpy.plots): court_rates (the measured rate of a column in each court), cases_over_time and issues_over_time (cases, and cases with each issue, of every court in each year, a panel per court), panel_composition (the rate for each panel from "DDD" to "RRR"), lower_court_rates (the rate for each court below, by reviewing court), trends_by_administration (yearly rates with presidents' terms shaded), and judge_network. Courts are labeled with their feature_name.
    • Evaluators (courtpy.audits): audit_random_assignment (balance of case traits across panel compositions within court and year, with a permutation test), audit_parsing (cases missing what the rules should find, with examples), audit_coverage (cases against the Integrated Database's appeals by court and year), and validate_coding (agreement and Cohen's kappa with another source's coding).
    • Inferer (courtpy.inferers): panel_effects, the effect of the colleagues' party on a judge's vote, with court-year fixed effects, standard errors clustered by case, and a randomization test.
    • CI installs the analysis extra, so the tests of the plots run there and mypy checks against the same packages as development.
    • The README's coverage badge reads the main branch, since Codecov's default branch for the repository was still "master".
    • courts.court_labels labels each case's court in the table's order, and judges.republican_appointees and judges.panel_compositions describe each panel by its judges' parties.
    • Breaking: the drop_text cleaner is now drop_text_columns (coders.DropTextColumns).
    • With keep_text, the table keeps the text of every section of each case (such as party, history, judges, and opinion), not only the opinions and the Lexis-Nexis header.
    • parse_all (parsers.ParseAll) parses loaded cases again from their kept text, with each case's source's header rules, "general", and its "rulebooks" ("federal" unless set), and makes author, panel_size, concurrences, dissents, and word_count again. It replaces amos's parse_all in the amos library and does what it does when given "rules".
    • courts.court_table reads the table of courts of any jurisdiction, and courts.court_names_by_courtlistener_name maps CourtListener's ids to courtpy's names for the courts.
    • Breaking: the CourtListener source is named "courtlistener" (it was "court_listener"), and so is the default folder of its cases. The load_court_listener loader keeps its name.
    • courtpy requires amos 0.10.2, for its savers.
    • courtpy.datasets has the kinds of amos.Dataset that courtpy makes, by what each row is: Cases, Dockets, Votes, Judges, Courts, CaseTypes, and PartyTypes (all importable from courtpy). Each names the column that labels its rows, and save saves it whole (in a joblib file, with amos's save_data) and its data as a CSV, parquet, or Excel table, with several values in a cell written as "; "-joined text. load loads either file. Breaking: load_cases and save_cases are gone, and a CSV file no longer has a ".lists.json" file beside it.
    • The loaders make Cases (or the kind that the last coder makes, such as Votes), "save" saves the whole dataset beside the table, and "reuse" loads it, history and all. load_cases loads a saved joblib file as the kind it was. courtpy.code returns Cases for a table, and courtpy parse saves the whole dataset beside its table.
    • The shapers that change what a row is return the kind they make. Breaking: reshape_by_votes, reshape_by_panel, and reshape_by_judge are now cases_to_votes, votes_to_cases, and cases_to_judges (CasesToVotes, VotesToCases, and CasesToJudges). cases_to_judges keeps nothing about any one case: columns of text are left out unless "keep" or "how" names them (it used to keep text that was the same in each judge's cases).
    • Dockets has a row for each docket number of each case, labeled by "case_docket" (such as "4567890-2"), with "case_id" and "docket" (the row's one docket number), so each appeal of a case that consolidated several has its own row. CourtListener's "docket_id" is kept. cases_to_dockets and dockets_to_cases make dockets of cases and cases of dockets, and dockets_to_votes makes a row for each judge on each docket. vote_table takes "label", the column of the labels of its rows.
    • add_idb matches each row of Dockets by its own docket number only, so each appeal of a consolidated case gets its own record of the Integrated Database (in Cases, a case still gets the record of the first of its docket numbers that the database has).
    • cases_to_courts, cases_to_case_types, and cases_to_party_types (in the new courtpy.shapers) summarize cases by court, case type, and party type, with the means of their columns of numbers and booleans (so the share of each court's or judge's cases of each case type and with each party type), and "by" for more columns to group by. Party types come from "party1_type" and "party2_type", which rules for them can make.
    • code_case_type adds "type_civil" (every case that is not criminal) and "type_{prefix}" for each other prefix of the rules of the criminal, civil, and constitutional files (courtpy.rules.case_type_prefixes): "type_tort", "type_contract", "type_ip", "type_writ", and "type_constitutional", and any prefix added later. Breaking: the "torts" rulebook is gone; its rules are in "civil", with the prefixes "tort_" and "contract_".
    • Breaking: the mergers are named add_...: merge_biographies, merge_scdb, and merge_idb are now add_biography, add_scdb, and add_idb. In Cases, add_biography numbers its columns by each judge's seat on the panel ("judge_name1", "judge_nid1", "judge_party1", ...), and its "lookup" can be a function that takes the place of the roster. code_panels numbers its columns the same way ("judge_name1" and "judge_nid1", which were "judge1" and "judge1_nid").
    • New mergers: add_jcs (each judge's Judicial Common Space score), add_executive (executive_name and executive_ideology), add_legislature (senate_ideology and house_ideology, the chamber medians of the Congress then sitting), add_high_court (high_court_ideology, the median justice's Martin-Quinn score in the term then sitting), add_circuit (the median and total of the appointing presidents' parties and of the Judicial Common Space scores of the active, non-senior judges of each case's circuit on the day it was decided), and add_judge_scores and add_court_scores (scores from training data, in the new courtpy.scores). The first four match each case's date, not its year. Breaking: add_politics is gone; build_table still makes a table of years, which merge_keys can add by "year".
    • The table of federal courts is "instructions/federal/courts.csv" again, with each court's circuit and CourtListener id (read by the new courtpy.courts), and the presidents' terms are "instructions/federal/presidents.csv" (read by courtpy.politics.executives). Breaking: options._COURTS, options._TRIAL_COURTS, and options._PRESIDENTS are gone.
    • The README has a table of courtpy's techniques and datasets.

    0.4.1¶

    • cases.select_whole_cases raises a ValueError that names the query when the query does not say whether each row is selected (such as "year + 1"), in place of pandas' "Need to pass bool-like values".

    0.4.0¶

    • courtpy requires amos 0.10.1, chrisjen 0.5.0, fjc-idb 0.0.4, and nagata 0.2.1. amos brings pyarrow and indexed-bzip2, so the "parquet" extra is gone: parquet files always work.
    • courtpy uses amos's file tools in place of its own. Breaking: utilities.download_file, utilities.page_links, and utilities.html_to_text are gone; use amos.loaders.download_file, amos.loaders.page_links, and amos.mungers.html_to_text. utilities.open_session stays, to identify courtpy to servers.
    • BulkData reads the bulk files with amos.loaders.read_chunks, which decompresses and parses them on every core and drops the rows of other courts and cases before they become Python objects. BulkData.chunks yields chosen rows and columns of a file as tables, and BulkData.rows takes the same columns and keep. BulkData.fetch downloads with amos.loaders.download_file, which resumes a stopped download, and no longer asks the bucket for the file's size first.
    • The Supreme Court Database's files are downloaded with amos.loaders.download_file, which checks that each archive has the right file before saving it.
    • The modules are in a folder for each part, as amos keeps its modules in a folder for each stage: core (cases, options, secrets, and utilities), sources (courtlistener, bulk, and lexis), wrangler (rules, parsers, coders, and loaders), and references (judges, scdb, fjc_idb, jcs, and politics). Each can still be imported from courtpy itself (such as courtpy.judges), but its full name has changed (such as courtpy.references.judges), and so have the names of the modules' loggers (such as courtpy.wrangler.parsers). The tests are in the same folders.
    • Breaking: the data folder is "courts" in the data folder of the amos workspace (options._DATA_SUBFOLDER), as every amos project keeps its data, whether courtpy runs from its repository or is installed. options._DATA_FOLDER is gone. COURTPY_DATA_DIR still names another folder.
    • Breaking: the agencies of "agencies" are separated by "; " (not ", "), as every other column with several values in a case is, and split_values splits "agencies" by default.
    • Breaking: courtpy's rulebooks are made by rules.register_rulebooks from the folders of rule files, and are in rules.RULEBOOKS by name (such as rules.RULEBOOKS["federal"]). The classes ParseFederal, ParseCivil, and the rest are no longer attributes of courtpy.rules, but every rulebook and technique keeps its name ("federal", "parse_civil", and so on). A study makes rulebooks of its own folders the same way, so its settings can name them (rulebooks = federal, review), with a prefix for another jurisdiction's topics and a combined rulebook of them all.
    • The "review" rulebook (federal/review.csv, part of "federal") finds the kind of review of an appeal ("review_district", "review_agency", and "review_immigration") and the district judge of an appeal from a district court ("district_text" and "district_judges"). The "general" rules find three more standards of review: "standard_substantial_evidence", "standard_plain_error", and "standard_named" (words that announce a standard). These rules come from coa_judicial_behavior's "review.csv".
    • courtpy.cases has helpers for the columns of case tables that studies had each written: split_cell, column_as_flags, flags_as_numbers, select_whole_cases, and require_columns. courtpy's own techniques use them in place of their private copies.
    • column_as_flags (formerly the coders' private _flags) reads "TRUE" and "FALSE" in text columns as pandas 3 types them, so coders of a table loaded from a CSV file read its flags.
    • utilities.is_none_setting says whether a setting such as "none" or "false" turns something off, in place of two private copies.
    • courtpy judges makes and saves the roster of judges (with "--update", "--no-jcs", "--martin-quinn", "--rulebook", and "--output"). cli.main takes a program name and a function that adds a study's own commands, so a study's program has courtpy's commands and its own, and errors from downloads are shown as one line.
    • Sources and ways to download can be added by a study. A courtpy.Source reads saved cases, and a courtpy.Download collects them into a folder; each adds itself to courtpy.sources.SOURCES or courtpy.sources.DOWNLOADS when it is subclassed, as amos techniques do. courtpy's own are CourtListenerSource, LexisNexisSource, BulkDownload ("bulk"), and ApiDownload ("api"). A new courtpy.SourceLoader downloads (if asked) and parses any source, and load_court_listener and load_lexis_nexis are built on it, so a study's "download" (such as "opinions") needs no loader of its own, and a loader for a new source needs only its name. Options that a loader does not know (such as "parties") go to the way to download.
    • Breaking: parsers.Source and parsers.SOURCES are gone (see courtpy.sources), and so is options._SOURCES. courtpy download has a subcommand for each way to download, with its own options (such as courtpy download bulk --stream); "--courts" is no longer required by the command itself, and "--folder" defaults to the name of the source. utilities.to_date and utilities.to_integer replace the loaders' private _date and _integer.
    • courtpy.judges is a folder of private modules (_names, _roster, _biographies, and _panels), with the same public names.
    • The documentation has a guide to building a study on courtpy and a catalog of its techniques and of the columns of each rulebook, which is built from courtpy itself.
    • Strings use single quotes throughout, lines of code are at most 80 characters, and every file ends with a single newline.
    • The rules in the "instructions" folder are divided by jurisdiction. The "general" folder has the rules that fit opinions of any jurisdiction, and the "federal" folder has only the rules that fit federal opinions alone.     0.2.2¶

    Updated to amos 0.2.6, whose mergers and shapers join tables whose rows are different things. courtpy uses them to add judges, the politics of each year, and the Supreme Court Database to cases, and downloads other researchers' data from its sources.

    The federal rules now read what recent CourtListener cases have only in the text of their opinions. CourtListener's full case name, judges, and disposition are blank for about nine in ten of the federal appellate cases filed since 2025, so the parties' roles and the panel were unknown, and reversals were coded from the opinion's words anywhere. In a sample of 1,500 such criminal appeals, the roles are now found in 97%, and a panel of three judges in 99%.

    Added¶

    • The federal rules find the caption in the opinion's opening (caption, caption1, and caption2) when the header's caption names no appellant or appellee, and code party1_appellant, party1_appellee, party2_appellant, and party2_appellee from it. A caption is a role before "v." ("Plaintiff-Appellee, v."), or parties in capital letters with one role, so a citation in the opinion's words is not mistaken for one.
    • The federal rules find the decision that an opinion states (decision: "AFFIRMED.", "VACATED AND REMANDED.", "Affirmed by unpublished per curiam opinion", or "we reverse", but not "we will reverse only if") and code decision_affirm, decision_reverse, decision_vacate, decision_remand, and decision_dismiss from it.
    • courtpy.judges identifies the judges on each case, which the earlier, unreleased courtpy did and this version had not rebuilt. A Roster (Roster.from_fjc()) is made from the Federal Judicial Center's files of judges' service, demographics, and careers, which download gets, and matches a name in an opinion to a judge of the case's court, or else of a district court in its circuit, or else of another court, among the judges serving in the case's year. The careers are coded by the rules in instructions/judges.csv. Unlike the earlier version, a name that fits more than one judge is left unmatched instead of being given to one of them, no judge is matched outside the judge's years of service, a judge who was reassigned to another court keeps the appointment of the judge's own earlier service, a rating or vote that is not recorded is missing instead of zero, the district courts of Vermont are in the Second Circuit, and a clerkship at a district court does not make a judge a prosecutor.
    • merge_judges (an amos.Merger) adds what the roster says about the judge that each row names, such as the author of each opinion, with a prefix: the judge's attributes, and the judge's age, senior status, and whether the judge sat by designation in the case's year and court. Like every merger, it takes "source", "columns", "prefix", and "indicator", never adds or removes a row, and records how many rows it matched.
    • code_judges (a munger) adds the judges of each panel who were found (panel_names and panel_found) and the panel's composition: the mean of each attribute of its judges, such as panel_party, panel_woman, panel_age, and panel_designated. It is built from the lists_to_rows shaper of amos, merge_judges, and the merge_summary merger.
    • judge_votes (an amos.Shaper) and courtpy.judges.vote_table make a table with one row for each judge on each case, with the judge's attributes (judge_{attribute}), those of the other judges of the panel (colleagues_{attribute}), and the judge's vote on each outcome (vote_reversal and so on: the outcome, or its opposite for a judge who dissented). Like every shaper, it takes "label", "task", and "groups" for the table that it makes, and must come before the data is split. It can be one of a loader's coders, so a vote can be the label of a study, and load_cases reads the case_id column of a saved table of votes as text, so a study splits its cases the same way when the table is reused.
    • courtpy.politics and code_politics add the political context of each case's year: the party of the president and, from a table that build_table makes, the Supreme Court's Martin-Quinn scores and the median NOMINATE scores of each chamber of Congress. code_politics is the merge_keys merger of amos with the year as its key, so it takes that merger's parameters ("source", "on", "other_on", "columns", "prefix", "indicator", and "duplicates"), and a table with a year twice stops a study instead of quietly using one of its rows. A term of the court that the scores divide in two (as 2005 is) is one year, which the earlier version's table had twice.
    • courtpy.politics.download gets the current files of Martin-Quinn scores (from the links of https://mqscores.wustl.edu/measures.php) and of the ideology of members of Congress (the "Member Ideology" data of https://voteview.com/data), and build_table, martin_quinn, and nominate use them when they are given no files. courtpy.politics.justices returns the Martin-Quinn score of each justice in each term.
    • courtpy.scdb adds the Supreme Court Database. download gets the latest release from https://scdb.la.psu.edu/data/, cases and votes return its case centered and justice centered tables, and code_scdb (an amos.Merger) adds its coding to parsed cases of the Supreme Court (scdb_issueArea, scdb_decisionDirection, and so on), matched by the database's id of each case or else by a citation. CourtListener cases of the Supreme Court have that id as scdb_id.
    • examples/judge_votes.ini is a study of how judges vote in criminal appeals.

    0.1.0¶

    The first release, and the first on PyPI. It is a complete rewrite of the earlier, unreleased courtpy, which was built on siMpLify. The analysis is now done with amos (and its workflows with chrisjen). The changes below are from that earlier code.

    Added¶

    • Downloads from CourtListener, now the main source of opinions:
    • BulkData extracts cases from CourtListener's quarterly bulk data, which needs no API key and has no limits. It downloads the files (resuming an interrupted download) or streams them.
    • CourtListener downloads cases with the REST API (version 4). It spaces its requests, waits when CourtListener asks it to, and saves its place so that a download stopped by an account's limit resumes where it stopped.
    • Both save each case as a JSON file, which courtlistener.read_case reads. CourtListener's header information comes from its data, so no rules are needed to find it.
    • courtpy.secrets keeps the CourtListener API key outside of any project: in the system keyring, in a private file in the user's configuration folder, in the COURTLISTENER_API_KEY environment variable, or in a .env file.
    • A single rule engine (Rule and Rulebook) for every CSV file of rules, with one set of columns (target, variable, kind, pattern, value, ignorecase, dotall, and note) and nine kinds of rules. See src/courtpy/instructions/README.md.
    • The coders code_parties, code_case_type, code_outcome, and drop_text, which are amos techniques and can be named in any amos settings.
    • courtpy.Project, an amos.Project whose "cases" section collects, parses, and codes the cases before the analysis.
    • The courtpy command (key, download, split, parse, rules, and run).
    • Tests, and an example study (examples/federal_appeals.ini).
    • The parts of the snickerdoodle template: GitHub Actions (tests on Linux, macOS, and Windows with Python 3.11 to 3.14, linting, type checking, documentation deployed to GitHub Pages, releases, publishing to PyPI, and validating the Codecov settings), dependabot, issue templates, pre-commit hooks, an editor configuration, a code of conduct, a contribution guide, and documentation built with MkDocs (a tutorial, an advanced user guide, a guide to writing rules, recipes, and the API reference).

    Changed¶

    • The package uses a src layout and is built with hatchling and uv.
    • The old instruction files were converted to the new columns: organizer_lexis_nexis.csv and separate_opinions.csv became lexis_nexis.csv, keywords_federal.csv became federal/header.csv, and parser_opinions_federal.csv became federal/opinion.csv (and is now saved in UTF-8). Some variables were renamed to be easier to read, such as party_appnt1 to party1_appellant, counsel_us_atty to counsel_us_attorney, notice_unpub_rule to notice_unpublished, and disposition_op_reversed to opinion_reverse.
    • A decision is coded as a reversal from its stated disposition when it has one, and only otherwise from the opinion's words. (Before, either one was enough, so "we will not reverse" in an affirmed case counted as a reversal.)
    • Which party won an appeal is now coded from the party's role: an appellant wins a reversal, and an appellee wins an affirmance. (The old code had the two reversed.) Outcomes that cannot be known are missing values rather than false.
    • Judges' names are split at whole words only, so names such as "Kirby" and "Holland" are no longer cut at "BY" and "AND".

    Fixed in the rules¶

    • Fed_App_P, Fed_Civ_P, Fed_Crim_P, and Fed_Evid required a "§" sign, which is not used when federal rules are cited, so they never matched. The evidence rule's pattern was also malformed.
    • references_case now includes the Federal Reporter, Fourth Series (F.4th).
    • amend8 searched for the Seventh Amendment's numeral ("VII").
    • titlevii required the text "Title VII.," followed by a space.
    • alcohol searched for "ddistilled".
    • The opinion's dispositions (opinion_reverse, opinion_vacate, opinion_remand, and opinion_dismiss) only matched "We willreverse" (with no space) or a capitalized "Dismiss".
    • The variables criminal_manslaugther and general_aritcleiv are now criminal_manslaughter and general_articleiv.
    • Non-breaking spaces in patterns now match any space.
    • Lexis-Nexis header sections that end the header (such as a "JUDGES:" paragraph just before "OPINION") are now found.
    • Agencies are matched from the longest name down, so "Postal Service Board of Contract Appeals" is no longer labeled "Postal Service".

    Removed¶

    • The siMpLify-based modules (almanac, cookbook, entities, implements, cases.py, and controller.py) and the settings files that went with them.
    • The federal_archive instruction files, which the combined files had replaced.
    • The external data used to add judges' biographies and political context is kept in reference/, but is not yet used by the new version.
